02 — The logo

One mark, three colours

The mark takes the colour of the text around it. There is no separate white file to keep track of — ink, white, or the single grey for logo walls.

Clear space

A quarter of the mark's width on every side. Nothing enters that band — no type, no rule, no photo edge, no other logo.

Smallest size

24 px on screen, 8 mm in print. Below that the carved corners close up and it reads as a blob.

Ink on cream. The default, everywhere.

03 — The logo

Four ways to break it

The geometry is the identity. Scale it, recolour it to one of the three approved fills, and otherwise leave it alone.

✕

Never rotate. The mark already implies motion — turning it just looks like a mistake.

✕

Never stretch. Hold shift. The artboard is square and stays square.

✕

Never recolour. No gradients, no brand orange fill, no client colours.

✕

Never box or shade it. No holding shape, no drop shadow, no glow, no 3D.

Also: don't redraw it by hand, don't trace it from a screenshot, and don't drop it onto a busy photo — put it on a plain area that clearly contrasts with the fill you picked.

04 — Colour

One accent, used rarely

Roughly 70% warm surface, 25% ink and grey, 5% orange. If two things on a layout are orange, one of them is wrong.

Three rules

  1. One orange element per layout — the button, or the accent word, not both.
  2. Never set body copy in orange. On cream it's unreadable at normal size.
  3. Never add a second accent colour for a “secondary” action. Secondary means less weight, not a new hue.

05 — Typography

Two faces, one job each

Switzer carries everything. Reckless Neue appears once per layout, on the phrase that matters — always in deep orange.

Rules

  • One serif accent per layout. Two is one too many.
  • No all-caps sentences. Uppercase is for small labels only.
  • Keep lines to 60–75 characters so copy stays readable.
  • If neither font is available (a partner deck, an email client), fall back to system sans — never substitute a lookalike display font.

06 — Voice

Say the specific thing

Direct, plain, backed by a number. We sound like people who have actually shipped the thing — not like a brochure.

✓ Write this

“A landing page ships in 7–10 business days.”

✓ Write this

“Book a 30-minute call. No pitch decks, no commitment.”

✓ Write this

“On final payment, the code, designs and data belong to you.”

✕ Not this

“Lightning-fast turnaround times tailored to your needs.”

✕ Not this

“Let's explore how we can synergize on your digital transformation journey.”

✕ Not this

“Industry-leading, client-first ownership terms.”

Never use these words

leverage · synergy · seamless · cutting-edge · game-changing · revolutionary · best-in-class · robust solutions · unlock · digital transformation journey

English and French are peers

French copy is written for French readers, not translated from the English. Same structure, same length, never machine-translated.

05

02

The mark is ink, white or grey. Never orange, never a gradient.

06

07

04

One orange element. The button or the accent word — not both.
